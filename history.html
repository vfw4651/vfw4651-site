Post History

Two Marines, a brewery, and a hall on Haven Avenue.

The name over the door

Nelson-Mack Narragansett

Our Post carries three names. Two belong to Rhode Island Marines who were killed in action in Vietnam: Private First Class George J. Mack of Cranston and Private First Class Daniel R. Nelson of Coventry. The third belongs to the Narragansett Brewery, where the Post held its first meetings and where most of its original members worked.

The Post was chartered in 1971, five years after Mack was killed and two years after Nelson. Both names appear in the earliest record of the Post we have found, a city license list from September 1978, and they have been over the door ever since.

Where we’ve met

From the brewery to Haven Avenue

The Narragansett Brewing Company brewed its first beer in Cranston in 1890, and for most of the next century it was one of the city’s major employers. When Post 4651 was chartered in 1971, it met at the brewery itself. Most of the original members worked there. Among the Post’s members was Bruno Gallo, union foreman at the brewery from 1951 to 1981 and an officer of the brewery workers’ union local, who served on the Post’s House Committee.

After several years the Post moved to larger quarters at 1196 Cranston Street. It was there by 1978, and still there in 1981, the year the brewery shut down and laid off its workers. The plant reopened for a few months in 1983, then closed for good.

By 1986 the Post had 120 members and was still growing. By that September its club license was listed at 7 Haven Avenue in Knightsville, the hall we still call home, and the Post dedicated the hall in November. City license lists show the address as the Cranston Social Club in 1978 and 1984.

November 10, 1986

Dedicating the hall on Haven Avenue

On Monday, November 10, 1986, after Cranston’s annual Veterans Day parade, nearly 200 members and friends gathered at 7 Haven Avenue to open the Post’s new headquarters and dedicate it to Mack and Nelson.

Both families were there: Frederick Mack, George’s brother, and Mary Nelson, Daniel’s mother. So were former Governor J. Joseph Garrahy, whose brother Vincent was Post Commander, Mayor Michael A. Traficante, and State Representative Irving Levin, whom the mayor honored with a local appreciation day for his work on veterans’ affairs.

Commander Garrahy told the Herald that the move had been a long time coming.

Through the years

The Post in the pages of the Herald

Smaller moments from four decades of the Cranston Herald.

1989

A stranger’s thank-you

A Navy man on active duty, a life member of a VFW post in California, stopped in to ask for directions while visiting Cranston. In a thank-you letter to the Post, printed in the Herald, he wrote that he and his wife “were treated to some of the greatest hospitality that we have ever experienced during our travels.”

1992

An Outstanding Senior

The city named Post member John J. Judge one of Cranston’s five Outstanding Seniors for 1992. The Herald noted that he helped provide meals to the needy during the holiday seasons and had spent years coaching youth baseball, football and hockey.

1992–94

The Over-50 softball team

Nelson-Mack VFW #4651 fielded a team in Rhode Island’s over-50 senior softball league. The 1992 season was a long one, with 2 wins and 16 losses by mid-July. By August 1994 the team was 18–8 and leading the league’s C Division.

1994

A national appointment

Vincent J. Garrahy, the Post’s commander in 1986–87, was named an Assistant Inspector General of the Veterans of Foreign Wars of the United States for the 1994–95 membership year by VFW Commander-in-Chief Allen F. Kent.

1995

Two who were at Pearl Harbor

Herald editor Steve Loewenthal sat down at the Post with Fred Morrissey and Timothy O’Brien. On December 7, 1941, Morrissey was on gangway watch aboard the USS Avocet and O’Brien was aboard the battleship USS Maryland, moored beside the Oklahoma. Both manned anti-aircraft guns. “They gave too much to be forgotten,” Loewenthal wrote.

2000

A send-off for Kosovo

Post 4651 joined other Rhode Island VFW posts in sponsoring a luncheon for soldiers of the 443rd Civil Affairs Battalion before they left to join the peacekeeping mission in Kosovo.

2008

A report from Washington

U.S. Senator Sheldon Whitehouse visited the Post in January to update members on veterans’ legislation and hear their concerns.

1996–2013

A neighborhood hall

The hall has served as one of Cranston’s polling places. The United Fly Tyers of Rhode Island met here every month in the late 1990s, and in 2013 the hall was the setting for a steak fry to benefit the Companion Animal Welfare Society.
