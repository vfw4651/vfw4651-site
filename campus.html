Haven Avenue, Cranston

A hall and two memorials

Our hall sits on Haven Avenue in the Knightsville section of Cranston. Next door is Thomas Moretti Square, and a block away is Anthony J. Sivo Park. Each one honors a Cranston man who did not come home: a Marine killed on Saipan in 1944 and a soldier killed in Vietnam in 1968.

The City of Cranston has asked the Post to look after both memorials, and we are proud to do it. Together with our own grounds, they make up the Post’s campus: a place for ceremonies on Memorial Day and Veterans Day, and for neighbors to stop and remember.

1 7 Haven Avenue

The Post

The Post has made its home on Haven Avenue since 1986. Out front, beside the flagpoles, a granite stone carries the names of the two Marines the Post is named for, PFC Daniel R. Nelson and PFC George J. Mack, both killed in Vietnam. Their story, and the Post’s, is on our history page.

Behind the hall, members are building a beer garden for the canteen: a new fence, whiskey-barrel tables and fresh planting beds. It is a work in progress, and it gets a little better every month.

Thomas Moretti Square

Next door to the hall is a small green with a granite monument, a stone bench and big shade trees. It honors Private First Class Thomas Moretti of Cranston, a Marine killed in the Second World War.

Thomas Moretti joined the Marines in 1942 and served as a telephone man in the 10th Marines, the artillery regiment of the 2nd Marine Division. He came through the battle of Tarawa in November 1943, and in June 1944 he landed on Saipan with “H” Battery of the regiment’s 3rd Battalion.

Before dawn on July 7, 1944, Japanese forces on Saipan made the largest banzai attack of the Pacific war. Several thousand men broke through the Army lines on the Tanapag Plain and fell on the 3rd Battalion’s howitzers, where the artillerymen fought on as riflemen. Thomas Moretti was killed that day. The battalion received the Navy Unit Commendation for its stand, and Saipan was declared secure two days later.

The square was dedicated on May 23, 1948, by Tabor-Franchi Post 2396, another of Cranston’s VFW posts, 23 years before our own Post was chartered. Caring for it now is our way of carrying on what they began.

3 A block away, off Florida Avenue

Anthony J. Sivo Park

A short walk from the hall is a neighborhood park with paths, tall maples, a playground, a basketball court and a ball field. At its corner is the P.F.C. Anthony J. Sivo Memorial Square: a brick plaza with a flagpole, a memorial stone and a bench in his memory.

Anthony Sivo grew up in Knightsville and studied at Roger Williams College before he entered the Army in 1967. He trained as an infantryman at Fort Dix, New Jersey, and went to Vietnam with Company A, 3rd Battalion, 39th Infantry, 9th Infantry Division, in the Mekong Delta. He was killed in action in Dinh Tuong Province on July 24, 1968. He was 21.

The City of Cranston built the park in his honor and dedicated it on Veterans Day 1991.

Nearby

The West Cranston Vietnam memorial

Anthony Sivo is also remembered on the West Cranston Vietnam memorial, where Phenix Avenue, Park Avenue and Cranston Street meet. Its bronze plaque honors six men of West Cranston who gave their lives in the war: Major Frank Bennett, 1st Lt. Carmen DeCubellis Jr., Sgt. Edward Perry, Sgt. Danny V. Scurfield, PFC George J. Mack and PFC Anthony J. Sivo. George Mack is one of the two men our Post is named for; his story is on our history page.
